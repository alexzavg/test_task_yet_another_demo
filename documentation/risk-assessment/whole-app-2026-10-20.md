# Risk Assessment: Pulse Fitness Join the Club (whole app)

_2026-10-20 · Techniques: Risk Matrix · Sources: codebase, Confluence (QAA-1 DoR/DoD page), Jira (QAA-1), Figma (`_test mockups`)_

## Summary

- **The safety net is thin.** Only 2 automated checks exist, on Desktop Chrome only, and none of the 57 designed test cases has run yet. A change to the signup flow can break it unnoticed.
- **The phone experience is the biggest visible gap.** Several phone-layout requirements (AC18, AC20) have no support in the current markup or CSS.
- **Validation shows errors too early.** Errors appear as soon as a field loses focus, against AC7, which adds friction to every signup.
- **The confirmation promises an email that never comes,** and a reload loses the pass. This needs a product decision.
- **Smaller spec drifts** (loading time, apostrophe) and the accessibility of custom controls need verification.

High zone: R1 to R3, covering the regression safety net, the phone layout and early validation errors. No risk is rare but severe.

## Recommended focus

1. Run a testing session against the 57 test cases on desktop Chrome and iPhone 15 emulation to confirm R2, R3 and R6, and log each confirmed defect against QAA-1 so the DoD5 defect check reflects reality.
2. Extend E2E coverage to the DoD4 critical paths (validation, loading, confirmation, reset) and add a phone emulation project tagged `@mobile`.
3. Fix the phone layout against frames 10 to 12, or get design sign-off on the differences, and close open questions Q1 to Q3.
4. Ask the product owner whether the "Confirmation sent to" copy should stay when no email is sent, and whether losing the pass on reload is acceptable.
5. Do the DoD6 keyboard and screen-reader pass on the plan cards, goal chips, level control and the empty-submit alerts.

## Gaps and assumptions

- R2, R3 and R6 come from reading the code. They are likely defects but are not yet confirmed in a browser.
- The Figma frames are flat images with no layers, so design comparison is visual only.
- The git history has a single commit, so there is no change-frequency signal.
- Jira holds only QAA-1 and no bugs, so there is no defect history to weigh.
